A relational framework for schools

Every student, known by name, known by story.

Every Student Known helps schools map the relationships that already exist in their buildings — and close the gaps for the students who've slipped through. Grounded in Danielle Paoni's work on relational connection and psychological safety, built for the realities of rural schools.

Why this exists

Every Student Known didn't start as a school-culture initiative. It started with a mental health counselor sitting across from students carrying real trauma, in a rural community with almost nowhere close by to send them for care.

When the nearest support is an hour's drive away, the adults already in the building become the safety net — whether the school planned for that or not. ESK is how we made that safety net intentional instead of accidental: broadening the circle of trusted adults, tracking who's actually known and who isn't, and treating that connection as protective. Schools using it have seen real movement in the indicators that matter most — attendance, disclosed risk, and students staying enrolled long enough to earn their diploma and change their own trajectory.

The framework

Dots of Connection

Every student in the building gets a row. Every adult marks what they actually know, across four categories — not a feeling, a fact. No lines, no claimed relationships. Just a clear, honest record of where every student already has a dot, and where they don't yet.

The four principles

  • Belonging

    Every student can name at least one adult in the building who would notice if they were gone.

  • Visibility

    Staff can see the whole sheet — not just their own caseload — so gaps don't stay hidden in one person's memory.

  • Consistency

    Connection is a practice built into the school week, not left to chance or a single advisor's memory.

  • Trust

    Psychological safety for students starts with psychological safety for staff — so both get tracked, not just assumed.

Companion student survey. Students complete a matching self-report survey across the same four tracking categories — Name & Face, Scholastic, Extracurricular, and Would Reach Out — so staff perception and student experience can be checked against each other, not assumed.
